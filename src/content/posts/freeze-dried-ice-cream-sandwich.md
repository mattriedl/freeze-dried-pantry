A freeze dried ice cream sandwich is a regular ice cream sandwich with the water removed, so it turns into a light, crunchy bite that won't melt. To make one at home, cut frozen sandwiches into bite-size pieces, freeze them rock solid on lined trays, and run a full cycle in a pre-cooled freeze dryer. Most batches take about 20 to 40 hours.

The chocolate wafers turn crisp, and the ice cream becomes airy and melts on your tongue. Below is the full method, plus how to tell it's done, how to store it, and how to make your own sandwiches from scratch.

## What a freeze dried ice cream sandwich is like

A home freeze dryer freezes food, pulls a deep vacuum, and gently warms the shelves. The ice turns straight into vapor without melting. What's left is the same sandwich, just dry and porous.

Here's what usually changes:

- **The ice cream** puffs slightly and turns crunchy, then creamy as it dissolves.
- **The wafers** lose their soft, cakey bite and turn crisp, a bit like a thin cookie.
- **The flavor** tastes stronger, because the water that diluted it is gone.

The texture is close to astronaut ice cream, but with the extra chocolate crunch from the wafers. If you want the basics of how a cycle works, start with our guide on [how to freeze dry food at home](/how-to-freeze-dry-food/).

## Store-bought vs homemade

You can buy freeze dried ice cream sandwiches, usually as foil-packed novelty snacks. You can also make them yourself from any brand you like.

| | Store-bought | Homemade in a freeze dryer |
|---|---|---|
| Flavor choice | Limited, often vanilla or Neapolitan | Any sandwich you can buy or make |
| Cost | Higher per ounce | Lower per batch once you own a machine |
| Convenience | Ready to eat | Takes a day or two of machine time |
| Shelf life | Long, thanks to commercial packaging | Shorter; best within several months |

Prices vary a lot, so check current prices before you compare. Store-bought makes sense for a one-time treat. Homemade makes sense if you already own a freeze dryer and want to run full trays.

## Best ice cream sandwiches to freeze dry

Most classic sandwiches work well. Some turn out better than others.

**Good choices:**

- Classic vanilla sandwiches with chocolate wafers
- Neapolitan sandwiches, for the classic three-stripe astronaut look
- Chocolate, mint or cookies and cream fillings
- Store-brand sandwiches, which often work just as well as name brands

**Use with care:**

- **Cookie sandwiches with thick, soft cookies.** Thick cookies dry slowly and may stay chewy in the middle.
- **Sandwiches with caramel or fudge swirls.** High-sugar ribbons often stay sticky.
- **Chocolate-dipped or coated sandwiches.** Chocolate coatings don't change much and add a lot of fat.
- **Very rich, premium fillings.** Extra butterfat can make pieces greasy and shortens shelf life.

Lighter, simpler sandwiches usually dry faster and store longer.

## How to freeze dry ice cream sandwiches step by step

### 1. Gather your supplies

- A home freeze dryer
- Ice cream sandwiches, kept in the coldest part of the freezer
- Parchment paper or silicone tray liners
- A sharp knife and a cutting board
- Freezer space for the loaded trays
- Mylar bags or airtight jars, oxygen absorbers and desiccant packs

### 2. Cut into bites

Freeze dried ice cream sandwich bites dry far better than whole bars. Cut each sandwich into squares about 1 inch across. A standard rectangular sandwich usually gives you about 6 bites, depending on its size.

- Unwrap a few sandwiches at a time and keep the rest frozen.
- Use a sharp knife and press straight down. Sawing tends to crack the wafers.
- If the knife sticks, run it under hot water and wipe it dry between cuts.
- If the ice cream starts to soften, put everything back in the freezer for 20 to 30 minutes.

You can freeze dry a whole sandwich if you want the full bar for show. Expect a longer cycle and check the center carefully.

### 3. Load the trays

Line each tray with parchment or a silicone mat. Lay the bites flat in a single layer, wafer side down, with a little space between them. They expand slightly as they dry, so don't let them touch.

How many sandwiches fit depends on your tray and sandwich size. Lighter trays dry faster and more evenly, so leave room rather than crowding.

### 4. Pre-freeze until rock solid

Put the loaded trays in a deep freezer for several hours, or overnight. The bites should be frozen hard all the way through.

This step matters more than any other. Ice cream softens at a lower temperature than plain ice because of its sugar and fat. Soft pieces can melt, bubble and fuse to the wafers once the vacuum starts.

### 5. Pre-cool the machine

Start your freeze dryer before you pull the trays from the freezer. Many machines can cool the chamber first, which usually takes about 15 to 30 minutes. Check your manual for your model.

Move the trays from the freezer to the machine quickly.

### 6. Run the cycle

Run a normal full cycle, and tell the machine the food is pre-frozen if it asks. If your model lets you set the shelf temperature, a lower setting, around 125°F, helps limit melting and puffing. Some people also add extra freeze time at the start.

### 7. Check before you unload

When the cycle ends, test the bites using the checks below. If any piece fails, add a few hours of dry time and check again.

## How long it takes to freeze dry ice cream sandwiches

Most batches take about **20 to 40 hours**. Times vary by machine, load and room humidity.

| Factor | Effect on cycle time |
|---|---|
| Small bites (about 1 inch) | Shorter |
| Light trays | Shorter |
| Whole or half sandwiches | Longer |
| Full, heavy trays | Longer |
| Thick cookie sandwiches | Longer |
| Warm or humid room | Longer |
| Lower shelf temperature | Longer, but fewer melted pieces |

Whole sandwiches can run well past the high end of that range. When in doubt, add dry time. Extra dry time won't hurt ice cream sandwiches, but leftover moisture will.

## How to tell when they're done

Fully dried ice cream sandwich bites should:

- Feel very light for their size
- Feel room temperature, not cool, in your hand
- Have crisp wafers that snap instead of bend
- Be crunchy all the way through, with no soft or gummy center

Break open the largest bite from the middle of the tray. That's the last to finish. If the center feels cold, damp or chewy, put the trays back in for more time.

## How to store freeze dried ice cream sandwiches

Freeze dried ice cream pulls moisture from the air quickly. Package the bites as soon as the cycle ends.

- **For snacking within a few weeks:** An airtight jar with a desiccant pack works well.
- **For longer storage:** Use Mylar bags with an oxygen absorber, sealed with a heat sealer.
- **Where to keep them:** A cool, dark, dry place. Heat speeds up fat breakdown.
- **Label every bag:** Note the flavor and the date.

Ice cream and chocolate wafers both contain fat, and fat can go rancid over time, even in sealed bags. Plan to eat homemade batches within several months. If they smell stale, sour or "off," throw them out.

Once a bag is open, reseal it quickly. On a humid day, bites left out can go soft within hours.

## Food safety tips

Freeze drying preserves food, but it doesn't make unsafe food safe. **Freeze drying does not kill bacteria.**

- **Store-bought sandwiches are the easy choice.** Commercial ice cream is made with pasteurized dairy.
- **Keep everything frozen while you work.** Don't let pieces thaw and refreeze. If they melt, put them back in the freezer before loading.
- **Use clean trays, knives and hands.** Wear gloves when you package finished batches if you like.
- **Toss pieces that never fully dried.** A soft center can spoil in storage.

## Freeze dried ice cream sandwich recipe (homemade)

Making your own sandwiches lets you pick the flavors and size them to fit your trays.

### What you need

- Thin, crisp-style cookies or chocolate wafer cookies
- Ice cream, slightly softened so you can spread it
- A small offset spatula or butter knife
- A lined baking sheet or freezer-safe pan

### Method

1. Spread an even layer of ice cream, about 1/2 inch thick, on the flat side of one cookie.
2. Top with a second cookie and press gently.
3. Set each sandwich on the lined pan and freeze until firm, at least a few hours.
4. Trim the edges if needed, cut large sandwiches into bites, and follow the steps above.

For a sheet-pan version, spread ice cream between two thin sheets of baked chocolate cake or cookie, freeze solid, then cut into squares.

### Homemade ice cream safety

If you make your own ice cream filling, use a safe base. Many custard recipes use egg yolks. Cook the base to 160°F, or use pasteurized eggs, and never use raw, unpasteurized eggs. Our guide to [freeze drying eggs](/how-to-freeze-dry-eggs/) covers egg safety in more detail.

### Neapolitan ice cream sandwich

To make a freeze dried Neapolitan ice cream sandwich, line a loaf pan with plastic wrap. Spread thin, even layers of chocolate, vanilla and strawberry ice cream, freezing each layer for about 30 minutes before adding the next. Freeze the block solid, lift it out, slice it into slabs, and sandwich each slab between wafers. Cut into bites before loading.

Keep the layers thin. Thick stacks dry slowly and are more likely to stay soft in the middle.

## Troubleshooting common problems

### The ice cream melted onto the tray

The bites weren't frozen hard enough, or the shelves warmed up too soon. Pre-freeze longer in a deep freezer, pre-cool the machine, and try a lower shelf temperature.

### The wafers are bendy or soft

The batch needs more dry time. Wafers hold moisture longer than you might expect, especially where they touch the ice cream.

### The middle is chewy

The pieces were too big or the trays too full. Add dry time now, and cut smaller bites next time.

### The bites turned sticky after storage

They took on moisture from the air, or they weren't fully dry. Store them sealed with a desiccant pack. Slightly soft bites can often be crisped with a short extra dry cycle.

### The bites look greasy

Rich fillings can leave an oily film. Blot them gently with a paper towel and eat those first, since greasy batches go stale sooner.

## Ways to enjoy them

Eat freeze dried ice cream sandwich bites straight from the bag. They also work well:

- Crumbled over cake, pudding, yogurt or a bowl of regular ice cream
- Packed in lunchboxes or road-trip snacks, since they won't melt
- Mixed into a sampler bag with other crunchy treats

Don't try to rehydrate them. Water turns them into a soggy mess instead of a creamy sandwich.
