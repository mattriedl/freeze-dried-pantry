Yes, you can freeze dry marshmallows, and they're one of the easiest treats to start with. Cut regular marshmallows in half (minis can go in whole), spread them in a single layer with space between pieces, and run a candy setting or a standard cycle with extra dry time. Freeze dried marshmallows come out light, crunchy and a little puffed, and they dissolve almost instantly on your tongue or in a mug.

Below is how to freeze dry marshmallows step by step, which settings work, how to tell when they're done, and how to fix chewy centers or sticky batches.

## What happens to marshmallows in a freeze dryer

A marshmallow is mostly sugar, corn syrup and gelatin, whipped full of air. Unlike hard candy, it also holds a fair amount of water. That makes it a true freeze drying candidate: the machine pulls that water out, and what's left is a dry, airy sugar foam.

Under vacuum, the air bubbles inside the marshmallow expand. With some shelf heat, the marshmallow softens and puffs before it sets. The result:

- **Texture:** crisp and crunchy, closer to a meringue than a chewy marshmallow
- **Size:** usually a bit bigger than when it went in, sometimes a lot bigger with warm shelves
- **Flavor:** the same vanilla sweetness, but it seems stronger because it melts so fast

If you've only tried puffing hard candy, our guide to [how to freeze dry candy](/how-to-freeze-dry-candy/) explains why some candies inflate while others barely change.

## What you need

- A home freeze dryer
- Parchment paper or silicone tray liners (marshmallows can stick to bare trays)
- Marshmallows: minis, regular, flavored or homemade
- Kitchen shears or a sharp knife, lightly greased or dusted with powdered sugar
- Mylar bags or airtight jars, plus desiccant packs or oxygen absorbers

## How to freeze dry marshmallows step by step

### 1. Pick your marshmallows

Any standard marshmallow works. Fresh bags are easier to cut and puff more evenly than stale ones.

- **Freeze dried mini marshmallows** are the simplest. They dry fast and evenly, and they're the right size for cocoa, trail mix and baking.
- **Regular marshmallows** work well cut in halves or quarters.
- **Jumbo or "campfire" marshmallows** are the slowest. Cut them into smaller chunks, or expect a long cycle and chewy centers.
- **Flavored and colored marshmallows** behave about the same as plain ones.

### 2. Cut large pieces

Cut regular marshmallows in half crosswise so each piece has a cut face. More exposed surface lets moisture escape faster, so the center dries before the outside over-puffs.

Sticky blades are the main headache. Dust shears with powdered sugar or cornstarch, or wipe them with a tiny bit of neutral oil between cuts.

### 3. Line and load the trays

Line each tray with parchment or a silicone mat. Place pieces in a single layer, cut side down or up, with gaps between them.

Marshmallows grow in the chamber. If they touch, they can fuse into one big sheet. Leave roughly a piece-width of space around regular halves, and a little room around minis. Keep the stack low so expanding pieces don't hit the shelf above.

### 4. Choose your settings

You have two common approaches. Times vary a lot by machine, load size and room humidity, so treat these as rough ranges.

| Approach | Pre-freeze | Shelf heat | Typical result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Candy setting / warm shelves | Often skipped | Warm (many people use roughly 135–150°F) | More puff, faster, usually several hours |
| Standard cycle | Yes, full freeze | Normal drying heat | Less puff, slower, can take most of a day |
| Standard cycle + extra dry time | Yes | Normal drying heat | Reliable crunch for big or cut pieces |

Warm shelves give the dramatic, oversized look many people want. A standard cycle keeps marshmallows closer to their original shape. Check your manufacturer's guide for candy settings and recommended heat limits on your model.

If you're running a mixed load, don't put marshmallows next to wet foods like fruit or meals on a candy setting. Those need a full pre-freeze and a different program.

### 5. Run the cycle and add dry time

Start the cycle and let it finish. Then add extra dry time, often an hour or two, before you call it done. Marshmallows hide moisture in the center, and a little bonus time is cheap insurance.

### 6. Test before you bag

Pull the largest piece and break it open. It should snap cleanly and feel dry and crisp all the way through.

## How to tell freeze dried marshmallows are done

A finished batch passes all of these:

- **Snap test:** a piece breaks with a crunch, not a bend
- **No gummy core:** the center is the same dry, foamy texture as the outside
- **Not cold:** pieces feel room temperature, not cool, when you take them out (coolness can mean ice is still subliming)
- **Light weight:** they feel almost weightless for their size

If even one large piece is chewy in the middle, put the tray back and add more dry time. Moisture left in one piece can soften the whole bag later.

## Freeze dried marshmallows for hot chocolate and more

Crunchy marshmallows have plenty of uses beyond snacking.

### In hot chocolate

Freeze dried marshmallows for hot chocolate are a favorite because they look great and melt fast. Drop them on top just before serving. They float for a moment, then soften into a sweet foam. If you want them to last longer in the mug, use larger pieces.

They're also a nice touch in homemade cocoa mix jars. Keep the marshmallows in a separate small bag or add a desiccant packet, since cocoa mix can carry moisture that softens them.

### Other ideas

- Stirred into trail mix or snack mix
- Crushed and sprinkled on cakes, cupcakes or ice cream
- Mixed into cereal or yogurt right before eating
- Paired with other treats from your freeze dryer, like [crunchy freeze dried ice cream](/freeze-dryer-ice-cream/) or [puffed Jolly Ranchers](/freeze-dried-jolly-ranchers/)

Add them at the last minute to anything wet. They soak up liquid quickly and go soft.

## A simple freeze dried marshmallows recipe with homemade marshmallows

Homemade marshmallows freeze dry well too, and you control the flavor. A basic freeze dried marshmallows recipe looks like this:

1. Make your favorite homemade marshmallow recipe and let it set fully, usually overnight.
2. Cut it into small cubes, about 1 inch or smaller. Dust with a mix of powdered sugar and cornstarch so the cut sides don't stick together.
3. Shake off extra dusting. A light coat is fine; heavy powder can scorch or look patchy.
4. Load trays with space between cubes and freeze dry as above, adding extra dry time.

Flavor ideas: peppermint, vanilla bean, strawberry or a little cocoa powder in the dusting. Keep add-ins dry. Swirls of jam, fresh fruit puree or chocolate add moisture or fat that can stop the cubes from drying fully and shorten shelf life.

## Storage and shelf life

Sugar is hygroscopic, which means it pulls water out of the air. A bowl of freeze dried marshmallows left out on a humid day can turn sticky within hours.

- **Bag right away.** Package them as soon as the cycle ends and they've passed the snap test.
- **Use airtight containers.** Mylar bags or glass jars with good seals work best.
- **Add a desiccant or oxygen absorber.** This helps keep them crisp and protects flavor.
- **Store cool, dark and dry.** Heat and light can fade colors and soften texture.
- **Smaller bags are better.** Each time you open a big bag, moisture gets in.

Sealed and stored well, plain freeze dried marshmallows usually keep their crunch for many months and often much longer. Marshmallows coated in chocolate or mixed with nuts or other high-fat ingredients don't store as long, because fat can go stale or rancid over time. Eat those within a shorter window.

## Store-bought vs homemade freeze dried marshmallows

You can buy freeze dried marshmallows online and in some candy shops. Prices vary a lot by brand and size, so check current prices before comparing.

Making them at home makes the most sense if you already own a freeze dryer. Marshmallows are inexpensive, they're easy to load, and you can run them alongside other candy. Store-bought bags are the easy choice if you only want a small amount or don't have a machine. ## Common mistakes and troubleshooting

### Chewy or gummy centers

The middle didn't dry. Cut pieces smaller, load fewer per tray and add more dry time.

### Marshmallows fused into a sheet

They were too close together. Next time, leave more space. You can still break the sheet apart and enjoy it as marshmallow bark.

### Pieces collapsed or look shriveled

This can happen if shelves get too hot too fast, or if pieces didn't fully dry before the vacuum released. Try a slightly lower shelf temperature or a standard cycle with a pre-freeze.

### Brown or scorched spots

Shelves were too hot, or heavy powdered sugar dusting browned. Lower the heat and use only a light dusting.

### Stuck to the tray

Always use parchment or silicone liners. If pieces still stick, let the tray sit a minute at room temperature, then gently lift with a thin spatula.

### Batch turned sticky in storage

Moisture got in. Either the bag wasn't sealed well, it was left open too long, or a few pieces weren't fully dry. Sticky marshmallows can often go back in the freeze dryer for another dry cycle before you reseal them.

### Food safety notes

Marshmallows are already shelf-stable, so they're low risk. Still, freeze drying doesn't kill germs, so start with clean hands, clean trays and fresh, unopened product. Keep homemade marshmallows covered while they set, and store finished pieces sealed. Marshmallows are a choking hazard for young children, and crunchy pieces are no exception.
