To freeze dry eggs, whisk raw eggs (or scramble and cook them), pour them onto lined trays no more than about half an inch deep, pre-freeze them solid, and run a full freeze drying cycle. Most loads take roughly 20 to 30+ hours. Once the eggs are completely dry, crumble or powder them and seal them in Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers.

Freeze dried eggs are one of the most useful things you can make in a home freeze dryer. They store for years, they're light, and they bake and scramble much like fresh eggs. Here's exactly how to do it.

## Raw vs scrambled: which way to freeze dry eggs

There are two ways to freeze dry eggs, and both work well.

| | Raw whisked eggs | Cooked scrambled eggs |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Baking, scrambles, any recipe | Quick meals, camping, backpacking |
| Prep | Whisk and pour | Cook, cool, spread out |
| After rehydrating | Acts like raw egg; must be cooked | Ready to eat, just add hot water |
| Texture | Smooth powder | Crumbly bits |
| Food safety | Must be cooked after rehydrating | Already cooked |

Most people with backyard chickens freeze dry **raw** eggs because one batch covers baking, breakfast and everything in between.

## What you need

- A home freeze dryer
- Eggs (fresh is best)
- A large bowl and whisk, or a blender
- Silicone tray liners or parchment paper, or tray lids with raised edges
- Mylar bags and oxygen absorbers, or airtight jars
- A blender or food processor if you want powder
- A marker for labeling

## How to freeze dry raw eggs step by step

1. **Whisk the eggs well.** Blend until yolks and whites are completely combined. Streaks of white dry at a different rate than yolk.
2. **Line the trays.** Silicone liners make removal easy. Raw egg can stick hard to bare trays.
3. **Pour, don't overfill.** A common guideline is roughly a dozen eggs per tray in a medium machine. Keep the depth under about half an inch so the middle dries fully.
4. **Pre-freeze the trays.** Freezing the trays solid in a chest freezer first keeps liquid from spilling and can shorten the cycle. Many people use tray lids or stack trays on spacers in the freezer.
5. **Load and run a normal cycle.** Use your machine's standard (non-candy) settings. Expect roughly 20 to 30 hours or more, depending on load size, room temperature and humidity.
6. **Check for dryness.** Eggs are done when they're light, crisp and break apart easily with no cool, damp or chewy spots. Press the center of the thickest piece. If in doubt, add extra dry time.
7. **Powder it (optional).** Pulse the dried egg in a blender into a fine freeze dried egg powder. Powder packs tighter and measures more easily.
8. **Package right away.** Seal in Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers or airtight jars as soon as possible so the eggs don't pick up moisture.

## How many eggs fit on a freeze dryer tray?

It depends on your machine's tray size. A common rule of thumb is **roughly a dozen eggs per tray in a medium home freeze dryer**, with fewer on small trays and more on large ones.

Depth matters more than the count. Keep liquid egg **no deeper than about half an inch**, and leave a little room at the edges so it doesn't spill when you slide the tray in. If you're unsure, measure how many whisked eggs fill one tray to that depth once, then write the number down for next time.

## How to freeze dry scrambled eggs

Cook scrambled eggs plain, with little or no butter, oil, cheese or milk. Fat shortens shelf life and can go rancid. Cool them, break them into small crumbles, spread them in a single layer on trays, then freeze dry on a normal cycle. Cooked eggs often dry a bit faster than raw because they're already broken into pieces.

## How long do freeze dried eggs last?

Packaging decides shelf life.

- **Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers, stored cool and dark:** commonly kept for years.
- **Airtight jars with an oxygen absorber:** good for long storage, but light exposure shortens it. Keep jars in a dark cabinet.
- **Opened bags:** use within a few weeks to a few months, and reseal tightly after each use.

Label each bag with the date and the number of eggs it holds. It saves a lot of guesswork later.

## How to rehydrate freeze dried eggs

A common ratio for one large egg:

- **2 tablespoons egg powder + 2 tablespoons water**

Stir well and let it sit for a few minutes until it looks like beaten egg. For scrambles, cook as usual. For baking, you can often mix the powder in with the dry ingredients and add the water to the wet ingredients.

Scrambled freeze dried eggs just need a little hot water. Add it slowly until they're soft, then let them sit a couple of minutes.

## Food safety tips

- **Freeze drying doesn't kill bacteria.** Raw egg powder carries the same salmonella risk as raw eggs. Always cook rehydrated raw eggs to a safe temperature.
- **Don't use raw egg powder in no-bake foods** like homemade mayonnaise, eggnog or raw cookie dough.
- **Use clean, uncracked eggs** and wash your hands, bowls and trays before you start.
- **Keep it dry.** If stored egg powder clumps, smells off or shows any moisture, throw it out.

## Common mistakes

- **Pouring too deep.** Thick layers leave a wet center that can spoil in storage.
- **Adding milk, butter or cheese to raw eggs.** Extra fat reduces shelf life.
- **Skipping the dryness check.** One damp tray can ruin a whole bag. When in doubt, add dry time.
- **Leaving eggs out after the cycle.** Powder pulls in moisture quickly. Package right away.

## Is freeze drying eggs worth it?

If you have chickens or find a great price on eggs, freeze drying turns a short-lived food into one that stores for years and takes little space.
