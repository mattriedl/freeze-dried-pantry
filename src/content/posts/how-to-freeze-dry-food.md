To freeze dry food at home, you prep the food into thin, even pieces, spread it on trays, and run it through a home freeze dryer. The machine freezes the food very cold, pulls a vacuum, and gently warms the shelves so the ice turns straight into vapor. A typical cycle takes about 20 to 40 hours, and then you seal the dry food in airtight packaging right away.

That's the short version. Below is the full home freeze drying process for beginners, from what you can freeze dry and picking your first batch to storing it so it lasts.

## How freeze drying works (in plain English)

Freeze drying removes about 98% to 99% of the water from food while keeping its shape, color and most of its flavor. It works in three stages:

1. **Freezing.** The food is frozen hard, usually well below zero °F.
2. **Primary drying.** A vacuum pump lowers the pressure in the chamber. Gentle heat from the shelves causes the frozen water to turn directly into vapor. This is called sublimation, and it skips the liquid stage.
3. **Secondary drying.** The shelves warm a bit more to pull out the last bound moisture.

Because the water leaves as vapor instead of liquid, the food doesn't shrink or toughen much. That's why freeze dried strawberries stay strawberry-shaped and crunchy, while dehydrated ones turn leathery. ## What you need to freeze dry at home

Here's the basic gear:

- **A home freeze dryer** with a vacuum pump. Home units come in small, medium and large sizes. Prices change often, so check current prices before you buy.
- **Trays and liners.** Silicone mats or parchment paper keep food from sticking.
- **Tray lids or dividers (optional).** Helpful for liquids and stacking trays in the freezer.
- **Mylar bags** and a heat sealer, or canning jars for short-term storage.
- **Oxygen absorbers** sized for your bags.
- **Labels** and a marker for the food, date and any notes.

A food scale is handy too. Weighing a tray before and after a cycle helps you track how much water came out.

## Step-by-step: how to freeze dry food

### 1. Pick and prep the food

Start with fresh, good-quality food. Freeze drying preserves food as it is; it won't improve old or bruised produce.

- Wash and dry fruits and vegetables.
- Peel anything with thick skins, or score skins (like blueberries and grapes) so moisture can escape.
- Cut food into pieces about 1/4 to 1/2 inch thick. Thin, even pieces dry faster and more evenly.
- Cook meats, eggs and meals fully if you want them ready to eat after rehydrating.

### 2. Load the trays

Line each tray, then spread the food in a single layer. Don't pile it up.

- **Solid pieces:** one layer, with a little space between pieces.
- **Liquids and purees** (soups, yogurt, sauces): usually no deeper than about 1/2 to 3/4 inch.
- **Mixed meals:** spread thin and break up big chunks.

Overloading is the most common reason a batch comes out damp in the middle. Check your machine's manual for its maximum load per tray.

### 3. Pre-freeze (optional)

Most home freeze dryers freeze the food for you as part of the cycle. Still, many people pre-freeze loaded trays in a chest or upright freezer for several hours or overnight. This can shorten the total cycle and frees up the machine faster.

### 4. Start the cycle

Slide the trays in, close the door and start the program. Most home machines run an automatic cycle that handles freezing, vacuum and drying on their own. Many also let you adjust settings like shelf temperature or extra dry time.

For most foods, the default settings are a good place to start. Some foods, like candy, use a different approach with warmer shelves and a shorter run; our guide on [how to freeze dry candy](/how-to-freeze-dry-candy/) covers that.

### 5. Check that it's fully dry

When the cycle ends, don't bag the food right away. Check it first (see the next section). If anything feels cool or soft, add more dry time and check again.

### 6. Package it quickly

Freeze dried food pulls moisture from the air fast. Package it within an hour or two, sooner if your kitchen is humid. Fill Mylar bags, add the right size oxygen absorber, squeeze out extra air and heat seal.

## How long does freeze drying take?

Cycle times vary a lot by machine, load size, food type and the temperature and humidity in the room. These are rough ranges for a full home freeze dryer load:

| Food type | Typical cycle time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Thin fruit slices, berries | About 20-30 hours | Score skins on whole berries |
| Vegetables (blanched or cooked) | About 20-30 hours | Cut evenly |
| Cooked meats, diced or shredded | About 24-36 hours | Trim extra fat |
| Soups, yogurt, purees | About 30-40+ hours | Keep layers shallow |
| Whole meals, casseroles | About 30-40+ hours | Break up dense pieces |
| Candy | Often just a few hours | Uses a special approach |

A warm room can slow things down because the machine has to work harder to stay cold. Many people find cycles run faster in cooler months.

## How to tell when freeze dried food is done

Properly freeze dried food should be:

- **Room temperature** all the way through, not cool to the touch.
- **Crisp or crunchy**, and it should snap or crumble when you break it.
- **Light** for its size.
- **Even in color**, with no dark, damp-looking spots.

Break open the thickest pieces from the middle of each tray. A cool, chewy or icy center means the food isn't done. Put it back in and add a few more hours of dry time.

If you're unsure, put a sample in a sealed jar overnight. If it softens or you see condensation, it needs more drying. Food that isn't fully dry can spoil or grow mold in storage, even in a sealed bag.

## Food safety rules for freeze drying

Freeze drying is a great way to preserve food, but it has limits. Keep these rules in mind:

- **Freeze drying does not kill pathogens.** Bacteria like salmonella and E. coli can survive the process. Treat freeze dried raw food the same as fresh raw food.
- **Cook raw meat, poultry, fish and eggs** before eating. You can cook them before freeze drying, or freeze dry them raw and cook them fully after rehydrating. Keep raw items on separate trays from ready-to-eat foods.
- **Handle raw foods cleanly.** Wash trays, liners and hands well, just as you would after handling raw meat.
- **Follow your machine's instructions** and current USDA food safety guidance for cooking and handling.
- **Don't freeze dry spoiled food.** It won't make it safe.
- **Keep high-fat foods short-term.** Fat doesn't freeze dry out, and it can go rancid over time.

Raw eggs are a good example. They freeze dry well, but the powder must be cooked fully after rehydrating. Our guide on [how to freeze dry eggs](/how-to-freeze-dry-eggs/) walks through that safely.

## What can you freeze dry? Best foods to start with

You can freeze dry almost any food that contains water. If you're new, start with easy foods that dry evenly and show you how your machine works. Here are the best foods to freeze dry first, plus a few more freeze drying ideas:

- **Fruit:** strawberries, bananas, apples, peaches, mangoes, blueberries (scored).
- **Vegetables:** corn, peas, diced peppers, green beans, cooked potatoes.
- **Dairy:** shredded cheese, yogurt drops, cottage cheese, sour cream.
- **Cooked meals:** chili, soups, stews, pasta dishes, rice dishes.
- **Cooked meats:** ground beef, shredded chicken, diced ham.
- **Eggs:** raw whisked or scrambled.
- **Snacks:** candy, ice cream bites, cooked pasta.
- **Herbs:** basil, parsley and other fresh herbs hold their color well.

Leftovers are a great choice too. Freeze dry single portions of family meals, and you'll have easy grab-and-go food later.

### Foods that don't freeze dry well

Some foods won't dry properly or won't store well:

- **High-fat foods:** butter, oils, mayonnaise, pure nut butters and very fatty meats. Fat doesn't dry and can turn rancid.
- **High-sugar liquids:** honey, syrups, jams and jellies tend to stay sticky.
- **Whole items with tough skins** that aren't scored or sliced, since moisture can get trapped inside.

Small amounts of fat or sugar in a mixed dish are usually fine. Just expect a shorter storage life for richer foods.

## How to store freeze dried food

Storage is where your work pays off. Moisture, oxygen, light and heat are what shorten shelf life.

| Storage option | Best for | Typical use window |
|---|---|---|
| Mylar bags + oxygen absorbers | Long-term storage | Years, when sealed and kept cool |
| Canning jars + oxygen absorber or vacuum seal | Foods you'll use in the coming months | Months |
| Zip-top bags | Snacks you'll eat soon | Days to a few weeks |

A few storage tips:

- **Use oxygen absorbers** sized to the bag. Open a fresh pack only when you're ready, and reseal the rest airtight.
- **Store in a cool, dark, dry place**, like a basement shelf or closet.
- **Label everything** with the food, the date and whether it's raw or cooked.
- **Reseal opened bags** tightly and use the food within a few weeks to a few months.

Some manufacturers say properly stored freeze dried food can last up to 25 years. Real-world shelf life depends on the food, how dry it got and how well it's sealed. Low-fat foods stored airtight in a cool spot usually last the longest.

## How to rehydrate freeze dried food

Many freeze dried foods are great as crunchy snacks. To bring others back to life:

- **Fruits and vegetables:** soak in cool water for a few minutes, or add them straight to soups, smoothies and baking.
- **Cooked meals:** add hot water a little at a time, stir and let sit for 5 to 15 minutes.
- **Meats:** soak in warm water or broth until tender, then use in cooked dishes.
- **Raw eggs and raw meats:** rehydrate, then cook fully before eating.

Add water slowly. It's easy to add more, but you can't take it back out.

## Common mistakes and how to fix them

- **Overloaded trays.** Food dries on the outside but stays wet inside. Fix it by spreading food thinner and adding dry time.
- **Uneven pieces.** Big chunks stay wet while small ones finish. Cut food to a similar size.
- **Bagging too soon.** Food that feels dry on top can still be damp in the middle. Always check the thickest pieces.
- **Leaving food out too long.** Freeze dried food soaks up humidity fast. Package it soon after the cycle ends.
- **Freeze drying fatty foods for long-term storage.** They can go rancid. Eat them sooner.
- **Skipping maintenance.** Vacuum pumps need care. Follow your manual for oil changes or cleaning, and defrost and dry the chamber between batches.

Once you've run a few batches, freeze drying becomes a simple routine: prep thin, load light, dry fully and seal fast. Start with easy foods like fruit and leftovers, then branch out as you learn how your machine handles different loads.
