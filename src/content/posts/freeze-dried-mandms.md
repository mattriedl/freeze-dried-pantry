Yes, you can freeze dry M&Ms, but don't expect every kind to puff like Skittles. Freeze dried M&Ms (sometimes written as MMs) usually come out with cracked shells and a lighter, slightly airy chocolate center, while caramel M&Ms freeze dried change the most and turn big and crunchy. Spread them in a single layer on lined trays, use a candy setting with moderate shelf heat, and pull them once a test piece is crisp and cool.

Below is what really happens to each type of M&M in a freeze dryer, how to set up the trays, which settings to use, how to tell they're done, and how to store them.

## Can you freeze dry M&Ms? What to expect

A freeze dryer works by pulling water out of food under vacuum. Candies that hold some water or trapped air, like taffy and marshmallows, puff up and turn crunchy. Candies that are mostly fat and sugar change much less.

Plain M&Ms fall into the second group. Milk chocolate is mostly sugar, cocoa butter and milk solids, with very little water. The candy shell is a thin layer of sugar and coloring. So when you freeze dry M&Ms:

- **The shell often cracks or splits.** As the candy warms under vacuum, the chocolate softens and expands a little, and the shell can't stretch.
- **The chocolate may get slightly airy.** Some batches end up a bit lighter and crumblier. Others look almost unchanged.
- **The flavor stays the same.** You're still eating chocolate and sugar.

That's why results online vary so much. A freeze dried M&M is more of a fun texture twist than a total transformation, unless you start with a filled variety.

If you're curious why some candies inflate and others don't, our guide on [how to freeze dry candy](/how-to-freeze-dry-candy/) explains the basics and lists the candies that change the most.

## Which M&Ms work best

Not all M&Ms behave the same. The filling makes the biggest difference.

| M&M type | What usually happens | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Caramel | Caramel center expands, shell cracks open, crunchy bite | Yes, the most dramatic |
| Plain milk chocolate | Cracked shell, slightly airy or crumbly center | Mild change, still fun |
| Peanut | Shell cracks, peanut stays about the same | Small change |
| Peanut butter | Filling may soften and spread, mixed results | Hit or miss |
| Pretzel | Pretzel stays crunchy, little visible change | Small change |
| Dark chocolate and other flavors | Similar to plain, little puff | Mild change |

Treat this table as a starting point. Recipes change over time, and limited-edition flavors may behave differently, so run a small test tray before you load the whole machine.

### Caramel M&Ms freeze dried

Caramel is the star here. Caramel holds more moisture than chocolate, and under vacuum it expands like a small balloon. The shell usually splits open and the caramel center turns light and crunchy instead of chewy. If you only make one kind, make this one.

Watch the shelf heat. Caramel that gets too warm can puff, then sag and stick. Lined trays and generous spacing help a lot.

### Freeze dried peanut M&Ms

Peanuts are mostly fat, and fat doesn't freeze dry in any useful way. The peanut inside stays about the same. You'll usually see cracked shells and slightly softened chocolate, but no big puff. They're fine to run, especially mixed into a tray with other candy, but they're not the reason people buy freeze dryers.

## What you need

- A home freeze dryer, ideally with a candy mode or adjustable shelf temperature
- Parchment paper or silicone tray liners (chocolate can smear on bare trays)
- M&Ms of your choice, fresh from the bag
- Mylar bags or airtight glass jars
- Oxygen absorbers or desiccant packs

## How to freeze dry M&Ms step by step

### 1. Line your trays

Cut parchment or lay a silicone mat on each tray. M&Ms are small and easy to spread out, but if chocolate softens or a shell splits, it can stick and stain bare metal.

### 2. Spread them in a single layer

Place the candies flat with a little space between each one. For caramel M&Ms, leave more room, since they can grow and touch. Crowded candies tend to fuse together into clumps.

A rough guide for tray loading:

- **Plain or peanut:** a single layer with small gaps
- **Caramel:** about a half inch or more between pieces
- **Mixed trays:** group each type together so you can judge results by kind

Don't pile them up. A second layer blocks heat and vapor and leaves the bottom candies unchanged.

### 3. Choose your settings

Most people run candy at room temperature without a pre-freeze. If your freeze dryer has a candy mode, start there. If you set shelf heat manually, a moderate setting is safer for chocolate than the hottest options used for hard candy.

Chocolate melts at fairly low temperatures, so very warm shelves raise the risk of smearing and melted puddles. If you set the temperature yourself, start lower than you would for hard candy and raise it in small steps on later batches. Check your manufacturer's guidance for candy settings on your model.

If your machine has no candy mode, a standard cycle with a pre-freeze still works. The candy will change less, and the cycle will take longer.

### 4. Run the cycle

Approximate times:

- **Candy mode with warm shelves:** often a few hours
- **Standard cycle with pre-freeze:** often most of a day or longer

Times vary a lot by machine, load size, room temperature and humidity. A light tray of M&Ms will finish far faster than a full machine of mixed candy.

### 5. Check before you pull them

When the cycle ends, take out one or two pieces and let them cool for a minute. Then break one open.

## How to tell freeze dried M&Ms are done

Because plain M&Ms don't change much, "done" can be hard to spot. Look for these signs:

- **Caramel M&Ms:** the center snaps cleanly and feels crunchy, with no chewy or sticky core.
- **Plain M&Ms:** shells are cracked and the chocolate breaks easily once cool. It won't puff like marshmallows, and that's normal.
- **No soft spots:** the pieces feel firm and dry, not tacky.
- **They're cool to the touch:** warm chocolate is always soft, so judge texture only after the candy cools.

If caramel centers are still chewy, add more dry time and test again. If plain M&Ms look the same as when they went in, a longer cycle usually won't help much. That's simply how chocolate behaves.

## Store-bought vs homemade freeze dried M&Ms

You'll find freeze dried candy, sometimes including M&M-style pieces, sold online and in specialty shops. Here's how the two compare:

- **Store-bought:** no machine needed, consistent results, but usually much pricier per ounce than a bag of regular M&Ms. Prices vary, so check current listings.
- **Homemade:** cheap per batch once you own a freeze dryer, and you can test any flavor you like. Results depend on your settings and take some trial and error.

## Storing freeze dried M&Ms

Chocolate is high in fat, and peanuts even more so. Fat can go stale or rancid over time, and freeze drying doesn't stop that. So freeze dried M&Ms won't keep as long as low-fat foods like fruit or vegetables.

To get the best shelf life:

- **Let them cool fully** before packing so no condensation forms in the bag.
- **Pack them quickly.** Sugar pulls moisture from the air, and caramel pieces can turn sticky fast.
- **Use airtight containers.** Mylar bags with an oxygen absorber are the best choice for longer storage. Jars with a desiccant pack work well for snacking batches.
- **Keep them cool and dark.** Heat can soften chocolate and speed up fat breakdown.
- **Plan to eat them within several months.** Check for stale or off smells, or a chalky or greasy surface, before eating older batches.

Freeze drying does not make candy shelf-stable forever, and it does not kill germs. Use clean hands and clean trays, and keep the finished candy sealed.

## Common problems and fixes

### Melted chocolate on the trays

The shelves were too warm. Lower the shelf temperature next time, use parchment or silicone liners, and space pieces farther apart.

### Candies stuck together

They were too close, or caramel expanded into its neighbors. Leave more space, especially for caramel M&Ms, and run smaller batches.

### Chewy caramel centers

The caramel still holds moisture. Add extra dry time and test again after it cools. Smaller loads also dry more evenly.

### No visible change at all

That's common with plain, dark and peanut M&Ms. Try caramel M&Ms for a bigger change, or mix M&Ms into a tray with candy that puffs well, like [freeze dried marshmallows](/freeze-dried-marshmallows/).

### Candy turned soft after a few days

Moisture got back in. Pack candy as soon as it cools, use a desiccant or oxygen absorber, and reseal containers right after opening.

## Ways to use freeze dried M&Ms

Freeze dried M&Ms work well as a crunchy snack, but they're also handy in other treats:

- Sprinkle crushed pieces over ice cream, yogurt or frosted cupcakes.
- Mix them into trail mix or snack mix with other freeze dried candy.
- Fold them into a batch of [freeze dryer ice cream](/freeze-dryer-ice-cream/) for a crunchy topping.
- Pack them in small bags for party favors or gifts.

For a colorful candy board, pair them with puffy freeze dried hard candy, which contrasts nicely with the chocolate.
